Sophie Moullin

Sociologist. Computational, demographic, interpretative, historical methods – and theory.

Articles & preprints

  • Modelling Mauri: Indigenous ideas and Aotearoa New Zealand's Covid-19 response Māori assertions of sovereignty and a biosecurity model established within New Zealand's science and policy institutions provided an alternative to biopolitics.
  • Submission to The People's Select Committee on Pay Equity A feminist critique of economics as it's used by centrist policy-makers, in the form of a parody.
  • The "dark matter" of social stratification Written with Michelle Jackson of Stanford. A review of the role of socio-psychological/ non-cognitive/ personality characteristics in social mobility and attainment; what economists identified as "dark matter" of human capital.
  • The role of unconditional cash benefits in child abuse and neglect Giving mothers of under-threes money unconditionally, and somewhat universally, reduced child neglect in New Zealand.
  • The single motherhood penalty as a gender penalty Comment, with Susan Harkness. A widely-discussed quantitative comparative analysis of poverty neglects gender, and the mutual construction of employment rates, household formation, and welfare states.
  • The human in human capital Dissertation. The subjective sense of self, as agentic or entrepreneurial, shapes social class reproduction, earnings inequality, and mental health in unexpected ways in the UK and US.
  • The character of class Preprint. Non-cognitive skills or character traits assessed at age 10 are consistent with gendered profiles of neurodivergence, and a mechanism of class reproduction in Britain.
  • Culture at work: self-entrepreneurialism and earnings inequality Preprint. It is partially true that a sense of self-entrepreneurialism determines earnings in the United States.
